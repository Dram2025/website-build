# Facebook & Instagram Ads Playbook: Stellar Haul Away + D.RAM Demolition

Two Fullerton businesses, one owner, one goal: **steady leads you can turn into jobs**.

> **The big idea:** Google Ads catches people who are *already searching*. Facebook/Instagram ads put your work in front of people *before* they search: the homeowner scrolling at night who has been meaning to clear out the garage or rip out that cracked patio. Meta wins on **visuals, price and convenience**, so every ad below is built around real job photos/videos and a simple offer.

---

## 1. Give each business its own lane

Running both businesses against the same customer wastes money and confuses people. Split them:

| | **Stellar Haul Away** | **D.RAM Demolition** |
|---|---|---|
| What it sells | Junk removal, garage/house clean-outs, furniture & appliance pickup, yard waste, light construction debris | Demolition, concrete/driveway/patio removal, interior strip-outs, block walls, heavy debris hauling |
| Typical customer | Homeowners, renters, landlords, realtors, estate clean-outs | Homeowners planning a remodel/ADU, general contractors, property managers |
| Job size | Smaller ticket, decided fast (often same week) | Bigger ticket, decided over days or weeks |
| Best Meta goal | **Many quick leads** (calls, messages, instant forms) | **Fewer, qualified leads** (forms with photos/size questions) |
| Budget to start | **$15–20/day** | **$15–20/day** |

Rule of thumb: if the job is mostly **stuff** → Stellar. If it's mostly **structure or concrete** → D.RAM. Refer jobs to each other.

---

## 2. One-time setup (about 1 hour total)

Do this once in **Meta Business Suite** (business.facebook.com) on a computer.

1. **Business portfolio:** create one portfolio (e.g. "Ramirez Ventures" or your company name). Both businesses live inside it, so you manage everything from one login.
2. **Two Facebook Pages + two Instagram business accounts**, one set per business. Same name everywhere: *Stellar Haul Away* and *D.RAM Demolition & Hauling*. Fill in phone, website, service area, hours, logo, cover photo.
3. **Two ad accounts** (Settings → Accounts → Ad accounts → Add → Create new): one per business. The same credit card can pay for both. Keeping them separate keeps each business's data and learning clean.
4. **Meta Pixel / dataset** (Events Manager → Connect data → Web):
   - **D.RAM:** create a Pixel and send Claude the **Pixel ID** (a long number). Claude adds it to dramdemo.com so every estimate request counts as a *Lead* in Meta.
   - **Stellar:** if it has a website, do the same; if not, use Instant Forms (below) and skip the Pixel for now.
5. **Privacy policy link (required for lead forms):** D.RAM can use `https://dramdemo.com/privacy/`. Stellar needs its own privacy page; Claude can make one.
6. **Lead notifications on your phone:** install the **Meta Business Suite** app and turn on notifications for *Leads* and *Messages* for both Pages.
7. **Don't pick a "Special Ad Category."** Home services/contractor ads are not *Housing* ads (that category is for home sales, rentals and mortgages). Choosing it would block useful targeting.

## 3. The campaign that gets leads (build one per business)

In **Ads Manager → + Create**:

| Setting | Choose |
|---|---|
| **Campaign objective** | **Leads** |
| **Campaign budget** | Advantage campaign budget **on**, $15–20/day |
| **Conversion location** | **Instant forms** to start (form opens inside FB/IG, easiest on phones). Add a second ad set later with **Messenger + Instagram + WhatsApp** (messages) for Stellar. |
| **Performance goal** | Maximize number of leads (switch to *conversion leads* once you've had 20+ leads) |
| **Location** | People **living in** Orange County cities you serve. Start with a ~15–20 mile radius around Fullerton, or list cities: Fullerton, Anaheim, Placentia, Brea, Yorba Linda, Buena Park, La Habra, Orange, Garden Grove, Santa Ana, Tustin, Irvine… |
| **Age** | 25–65+ |
| **Audience** | **Advantage+ audience**. Don't stack interests; Meta finds buyers better than manual targeting at this budget. |
| **Placements** | Advantage+ placements (Feeds, Reels, Stories on both apps) |
| **Ad schedule** | All day (people browse at night; you call them back in the morning) |

**3–5 different ads per ad set** (different photos/videos and headlines). Meta automatically pushes budget to the winner.

### Instant form setup ("Higher intent" type)

Choose **Higher intent**: it adds a review screen, which cuts down accidental and fake leads.

**Stellar Haul Away form questions**
1. What do you need hauled away? *(Garage clean-out · Furniture / appliances · Yard waste · Construction debris · Whole-home / estate clean-out · Other)*
2. Roughly how much? *(A few items · About a pickup truck load · Half a dumpster or more · Not sure)*
3. When do you need it gone? *(ASAP · This week · Within 2 weeks · Just getting prices)*
4. City / ZIP
5. Name, phone, email (pre-filled by Meta)
6. Optional: "Text us a photo for an exact price." Add your number on the completion screen.

**D.RAM Demolition form questions**
1. What are you looking to remove or demolish? *(Concrete driveway / patio / slab · Interior remodel demo · Garage / shed / ADU site prep · Block or retaining wall · Other)*
2. Approximate size? *(Short answer, e.g. "20 × 30 ft driveway")*
3. When are you hoping to start? *(Within 2 weeks · 1–3 months · Just planning)*
4. Are you a… *(Homeowner · Contractor · Property manager)*
5. City / ZIP
6. Name, phone, email (pre-filled)

**Completion screen for both:** "Thanks! We'll call or text you within 1 business day. Want a faster price? Text photos to [number]." Add a **Call** button.

## 4. Creative: what actually gets people to stop scrolling

Real work beats polished graphics. In order of what usually performs best for haul-away and demo companies:

1. **Before/after pairs**: the same spot, same angle. ("Packed garage → empty and swept.")
2. **Short vertical videos (Reels, 8–20 seconds)**: excavator breaking a slab, truck being loaded, a time-lapse of a clean-out. Add bold captions; most people watch with the sound off.
3. **You on camera (15–30 s)**: "Hi, I'm David, owner of ___ in Fullerton. Here's how pricing works…" Faces build trust fast.
4. **Simple offer graphic**: one clear line + photo + phone number.

**Shooting tips:** hold the phone vertically, shoot in daylight, take a before shot *every* job from the same spot, and keep license plates/house numbers out of frame (or blur them).

## 6. Speed wins the job

Most contractor leads go to whoever calls back first. With Meta leads that matters even more, because people often submit to several companies.

- **Call or text every lead within 5–15 minutes** during business hours. If you miss it, text right away:
  > "Hi [Name], this is David with [Stellar Haul Away / D.RAM Demolition]. Got your request about [job]. Can you text a couple of photos so I can give you a price? Or I can call you, whatever's easier."
- **Instant replies:** in Business Suite → Inbox → Automations, turn on an instant reply for Messenger/Instagram DMs with the same message.
- **Getting leads into your email/phone:** leads appear in Business Suite → **Leads Center**. To have each one emailed or added to a Google Sheet automatically, connect Zapier ("Facebook Lead Ads → Gmail / Google Sheets").
- **Track everything** in one sheet: date · business · source · job type · quote · won/lost · job $. After 30 days you'll know your real **cost per booked job**, the only number that matters.

## 7. First 30 days: what to check and when

| When | Do this |
|---|---|
| **Days 1–7** | Don't touch anything. Meta is learning. Just answer leads fast. |
| **Day 7** | Pause any ad with lots of spend and no leads; keep the rest. |
| **Day 14** | Look at **cost per lead** and, more importantly, **lead quality** (did they answer? was it a real job?). Add 2 new ads to replace the weakest ones. |
| **Day 30** | Compare cost per booked job against Google Ads. Move budget toward what books jobs. |

**Rough expectations** (Orange County, varies a lot by season and creative): junk removal leads often land around **$10–35** each; demolition/concrete leads usually cost more, around **$30–90**. No one can guarantee numbers. Judge by booked jobs, not clicks or likes.

**If leads are cheap but low quality:** switch the form to *Higher intent* (if not already), add one more qualifying question, or test sending people to the website instead.
**If leads are too expensive:** test new creative first (it's the biggest lever), then widen the radius.

---

## 8. After the first month: grow what works

1. **Retargeting ($5/day):** show before/after videos to people who watched your videos, visited your Page or Instagram, or visited the website. They already know you; this is cheap and converts well.
2. **Lookalike audiences:** once you have 100+ leads or customers, upload the list (Audiences → Create → Lookalike) so Meta finds similar people.
3. **Seasonal pushes:** spring cleaning and move-out season (May–Aug) for Stellar; pre-holiday remodels (Sept–Nov) and tax-refund season (Feb–Apr) for D.RAM.
4. **Reviews in ads:** once you have Google reviews, screenshot your best one and use it as an ad image.

---

## 9. Free organic posting (keeps the ads working better)

People check your Page before they call. An empty Page loses leads.

- **3 posts a week per business:** 1 before/after, 1 short video, 1 tip or offer.
- Post the same content to **Facebook, Instagram and Google Business Profile**.
- Join local Facebook groups (Fullerton, Brea, Placentia community groups) and **Nextdoor**: answer "who do you recommend?" posts and share helpful tips, not just ads.
- Ask every happy customer for a **Google review** the day the job ends.

---

## Rules that keep the ad accounts safe

- Use **your own photos**. Don't use stock images or competitors' work.
- **No fake reviews, no fake urgency** ("only 2 spots left!" when it's not true).
- Don't claim **licensed, insured or bonded** unless you are. Meta and customers both check.
- Don't target or mention personal characteristics ("Are you a senior?", "Divorced?"). Meta rejects ads that imply them.
- Keep your phone, address and business name **identical** across Pages, Google and websites.
